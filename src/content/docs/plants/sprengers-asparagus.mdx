Sprenger's asparagus is sold as a fern and is nothing of the sort — it is a
true *Asparagus*, cousin to the vegetable, and its "needles" are flattened
stems doing a leaf's job. The giveaway is underground: a mat of white
water-storing tubers that lets it shrug off a forgotten watering and, left
long enough, bend the pot out of shape. It is close to unkillable in bright
indirect light; harsh sun yellows it, deep shade thins it out.

A word of caution rather than medicine: the red berries that follow the tiny
white flowers are mildly toxic — a stomach upset for children, worse for
cats and dogs — and the arching stems carry small hidden thorns. Hang it
high; it looks best there anyway.
