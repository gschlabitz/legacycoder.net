## Origin

Sprenger's asparagus is sold as a fern and is nothing of the sort — it is
a true *Asparagus*, cousin to the vegetable, and its "needles" are
flattened stems doing a leaf's job. It comes from coastal South Africa, a
mild, frost-free biome where rainfall can't be trusted; the mat of white
water-storing tubers is its answer to that unreliability, and left long
enough it will bend a pot out of shape.

## Care

- **Light** — bright indirect to gentle morning sun.
- **Temperature** — 50–77 °F; no frost.
- **Watering** — every 4 days; moderately moist — the water-storing tubers
  forgive a missed round.
- **Fertilizing** — every 4 weeks, March–September; balanced liquid feed
  at half strength.
- **Trimming** — yearly, in March; cut yellowed or bald stems out at the
  base to force fresh fronds.
- **Repotting** — every 2 years, March–April; the tuberous roots will
  bulge or crack a tight pot — size up, or divide instead.

It is close to unkillable in bright indirect light — harsh sun yellows it,
deep shade thins it out.

A word of caution rather than medicine: the red berries that follow the tiny
white flowers are mildly toxic — a stomach upset for children, worse for
cats and dogs — and the arching stems carry small hidden thorns. Hang it
high; it looks best there anyway.

## Pruning

Cut yellowed or bald stems out at the base in March — fresh fronds push
from below, not from trimmed tips, so there is no shaping to do. The real
size control is underground: instead of sizing up forever, pull the root
ball as the first new spears appear, saw the tuber mass in half, and
return one half to the same pot. Those water-storing tubers carry the
recovery rations, so it shrugs off division that would stagger a lesser
plant.
