A practical year for the pots on this site, tuned to southern Illinois, running
from the beginning of the year to the end. Check the soil before watering and
the forecast before moving anything: heat, rain, pot size, and indoor light
matter more than a date.

## The watering rhythm

Keep these groups together when you make the rounds. In winter, stretch every
rhythm and let the soil—not the weekday—call the next round.

### ☀️ Daily in hot weather

Check every morning during midsummer heat. Keep evenly moist, never swampy;
tomatoes suffer when they swing from dry to soaked.

- [Sweet Basil](/plants/sweet-basil/)
- [Roma Tomato](/plants/roma-tomato/)
- [Husky Cherry Tomato](/plants/husky-cherry-tomato/)

### 💧 The moisture crew

Check every two or three days in active growth. Water when the surface just
begins to dry.

- [Coriander](/plants/coriander/)
- [Curly Parsley](/plants/curly-parsley/)
- [Wax Begonia](/plants/wax-begonia/)
- [Sprenger's Asparagus](/plants/sprengers-asparagus/)

### 🪴 Watering weekend

A weekly check is usually enough. Wait for the top few centimeters to dry,
then water thoroughly and empty the saucer.

- [Christmas Cactus](/plants/christmas-cactus/)
- [Citronella](/plants/citronella/)

### 🏜️ The dry-pot club

Let the pot dry right through. Soak fully, drain fully, and skip the round if
the mix still feels cool or damp.

- [Lavender](/plants/lavender/)
- [Rosemary](/plants/rosemary/)
- [German Thyme](/plants/german-thyme/)
- [Silver Squill](/plants/silver-squill/)

## The big annual moves

- **Mid-May** — Move pots onto the patio after frost risk has passed and
  nights stay above 10 °C. Harden them off over 7–10 days; basil and tomatoes
  wait for reliably warm nights.
- **Late September** — Start watching night temperatures. Bring basil, wax
  begonia, citronella, Sprenger's asparagus, silver squill, and Christmas
  cactus in before nights fall below 10 °C; tomatoes finish at frost.
- **March & April** — Repot root-bound plants before strong growth.
  Citronella, wax begonia, coriander, and parsley get fresh starts; asparagus,
  Mediterranean herbs, cactus, and squill only move up when their own cycle or
  roots call for it.
- **Autumn & winter** — Give Christmas cactus cool, dark evenings for buds.
  Keep silver squill bulbs high and dry, reduce all feeding, and inspect
  indoor pots for pests and rot.

## Month by month

### January — Rest and observe

_Deep winter_

- Keep rosemary cool and bright; barely water lavender and silver squill.
- After its bloom, let Christmas cactus dry a little longer between drinks.
- No routine feeding. Turn indoor pots weekly and check leaf undersides for
  pests.

### February — Prepare the next crop

_Deep winter_

- Late in the month, start tomatoes indoors if growing from seed.
- Sow parsley early; begin a small coriander pot and plan another every few
  weeks.
- Gather pots and gritty mix, but leave settled perennials alone until growth
  starts.

### March — The spring reset

_Early spring_

- Prune citronella hard and cut yellow or bare asparagus stems at the base.
- Repot wax begonia and citronella; resow parsley and coriander rather than
  nursing tired plants.
- Check asparagus roots, Mediterranean herbs, Christmas cactus, and silver
  squill. Repot only if due or crowded; divide squill with its bulbs left
  above the mix.

### April — Wake everything gently

_Spring_

- Resume light feeding as new growth appears: monthly for most leafy pots,
  only every other month for Mediterranean herbs and squill.
- Divide crowded supermarket basil and begin pinching once it branches.
- On mild days, start hardening patio plants in shade and bring them back
  inside at night.

### May — Move to the patio

_Patio season_

- After the last frost and once nights hold above 10 °C, move plants out
  gradually. Keep basil and tomatoes back until nights are reliably warm.
- Plant each tomato deeply in its own large pot; use at least 30 cm for Roma
  and 25 cm for Husky Cherry.
- Begin tomato feed after flowering or fruit set. Shape rosemary lightly after
  flowering—never cut bare wood.

### June — Build the summer routine

_Early summer_

- Pinch basil tips and flowers; harvest coriander and parsley from the
  outside.
- Feed tomatoes every two weeks. Give basil, coriander, parsley, begonia,
  citronella, asparagus, and cactus a half-strength monthly feed.
- Remove yellow lower leaves from Roma; thin Husky Cherry only enough for
  airflow.

### July — Heat rules the calendar

_High summer_

- Check basil and both tomatoes every morning; in extreme heat they may need
  a second evening drink.
- Give coriander and parsley afternoon shade and keep them evenly moist; resow
  coriander when it bolts.
- Trim thyme by one third after flowering, staying above green growth. Never
  leave any pot standing in water.

### August — Harvest and renew

_Late summer_

- Cut lavender back by one third after flowering and harvest the flower
  spikes; never cut into leafless wood.
- Keep pinching basil, tomatoes, and begonia. Continue tomato feed while they
  are producing.
- Take citronella, begonia, rosemary, or thyme cuttings as insurance and
  future replacements.

### September — Prepare the return indoors

_Early fall_

- Stop feeding Mediterranean herbs first; taper the rest late in the month.
- Inspect pots, rinse foliage, and treat pests before anything crosses the
  threshold.
- Bring tender plants in before nights fall below 10 °C. Take citronella and
  begonia cuttings if the parent pots are too large.

### October — Settle into indoor life

_Fall transition_

- Give Christmas cactus cool nights and uninterrupted darkness for 12–14
  hours; stop moving it when buds appear.
- Finish or compost basil and tomatoes when cold ends their season. Parsley
  can continue in a cool bright spot.
- Reduce watering as growth and light slow. Rosemary prefers a cool bright
  room, not a warm dim one.

### November — Follow the light

_Indoor season_

- Move indoor pots to their brightest suitable windows and rotate them weekly.
- Water only after checking the mix; the dry-pot group may skip several
  rounds.
- Remove dead foliage, keep leaves dry overnight, and continue the indoor pest
  watch.

### December — Bloom and winter rest

_Winter rest_

- Enjoy the Christmas cactus bloom without moving the pot; keep it away from
  heaters and cold drafts.
- Keep silver squill almost dry and inspect exposed bulbs for softness or rot.
- Plan next year's seeds, pot sizes, and which crowded plants truly need a
  March repot.
