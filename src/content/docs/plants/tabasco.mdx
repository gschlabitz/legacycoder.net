## Origin

A cultivar of *Capsicum frutescens*, named for the Mexican state of Tabasco; the species comes from the tropical Americas. Unlike most chilis it holds its pods upright above the foliage, ripening from yellow-green through orange to red.

## Location

Full sun, six hours or more, and real warmth; frost kills it. It runs on the tomato schedule — out once nights are reliably warm, done at frost — unless it overwinters on a bright windowsill: chilis are perennials and fruit earlier the next year.

## Watering

Evenly moist, never waterlogged, with daily checks in summer heat. Tomato feed every two weeks once the first flowers set, May to September.

## Pruning

Pinch the growing tips once while the plant is young — each pinch forks the stem, and more forks mean more flowers. After that, harvesting is the pruning; before overwintering, cut it back by about a third.

## Repotting

Yearly in May, one plant per pot of 25 cm or more.

## In the kitchen

Fiery, fruity, and prolific — the pepper fermented into Tabasco sauce, though fresh pods do the same work in salsas and marinades. One plant outpaces most households: string surplus pods to dry, or pack them in vinegar for a shake-on hot sauce.
