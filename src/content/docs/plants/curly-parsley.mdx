## Origin

Curly parsley is the ruffled cultivar of common parsley, bred for looks as
much as flavor. The species is native to the central and eastern
Mediterranean, growing through the mild, moist parts of the year on the
reserves of its deep taproot. It is a biennial: leaves the first year, a
flower stalk and seeds the second — by which point the leaves turn bitter.

## Care

- **Light** — bright, with a few hours of gentle direct sun.
- **Temperature** — 59–77 °F; tolerates light frost.
- **Watering** — every 2 days; keep the soil evenly moist, never
  waterlogged.
- **Fertilizing** — every 4 weeks, March–October; half-strength balanced
  liquid feed.
- **Repotting** — none; a biennial — most pots are resown each spring
  instead of repotted.

Give it a deep pot — the taproot goes further down than the frilly top
suggests — and expect sulking in exactly the midsummer heat that suits the
basil next to it.

## Pruning

Harvest from the outside in: new growth comes from the center of the
crown, and cutting into it stalls the whole plant. There is no renovation
pruning — a second-year plant bolts to seed no matter what you cut, so a
tired parsley is resown, not rescued.

## In the kitchen

Milder and less grassy than flat-leaf parsley, with a texture that holds up
as a garnish. Chop it into potato dishes, eggs, and butter sauces, or use it
as the base of a classic *Frankfurter Grüne Soße*. Add it at the end of
cooking — heat flattens the flavor quickly.
