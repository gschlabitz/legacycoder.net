## Origin

Despite the family name, this is no desert plant: *Schlumbergera* comes
from the coastal mountains of southeastern Brazil, where it grows as an
epiphyte in the moss of cloud-forest trees — a biome of filtered light,
humidity, and cool nights. It is a short-day plant, and the flower buds
are triggered by the long, cool nights of autumn.

## Care

- **Light** — bright indirect; no midday sun.
- **Temperature** — 50–77 °F; cool autumn nights set the buds.
- **Watering** — every 7 days, when the top of the mix is dry; sparser
  after flowering.
- **Fertilizing** — every 4 weeks, April–September; half-strength feed
  through the growing season only.
- **Repotting** — every 3 years, March–April; it blooms best slightly
  root-bound — don't rush this.

The December bloom is made in October: give it a spot with cool nights
(50–59 °F) and no evening lamplight, then stop moving it once buds show —
it drops them at any sudden change. Well-kept plants routinely outlive
their owners' other houseplants by decades.

## Pruning

No knife needed: after flowering, twist off a segment or two at the joints
to keep it branching — and every segment roots in moist mix, which is how
one plant becomes a windowsill dynasty. Leave the roots alone entirely: it
blooms best slightly pot-bound and can sit in the same pot for years, with
a fresh top layer of mix standing in for a repot.
