Despite the family name, this is no desert plant: *Schlumbergera* grows as
an epiphyte in the moss of Brazilian cloud-forest trees, which is why it
wants filtered light, some humidity, and watering more like a houseplant
than a cactus. It is a short-day plant — the flower buds are triggered by
the long, cool nights of autumn. That is the whole secret to the December
bloom: from October, give it a spot with cool nights (10–15 °C) and no
evening lamplight, then stop moving it once buds show, because it drops
them at any sudden change.

## Origin

*Schlumbergera* comes from the coastal mountains of southeastern Brazil,
where it grows as an epiphyte in the moss of cloud-forest trees — a biome
of filtered light, humidity, and cool nights. Every houseplant quirk
traces back to that perch: no desert treatment, no midday sun, snug root
quarters, and the long cool autumn nights that cue the bloom.

## Care

- **Light** — bright indirect; no midday sun.
- **Temperature** — 10–25 °C; cool autumn nights set the buds.
- **Watering** — every 7 days, when the top of the mix is dry; sparser
  after flowering.
- **Fertilizing** — every 4 weeks, April–September; half-strength feed
  through the growing season only.
- **Repotting** — every 3 years, March–April; it blooms best slightly
  root-bound — don't rush this.

Well-kept plants routinely outlive their owners' other houseplants by
decades.

## Pruning

No knife needed: after flowering, twist off a segment or two at the joints
to keep it branching — and every segment roots in moist mix, which is how
one plant becomes a windowsill dynasty. Leave the roots alone entirely: it
blooms best slightly pot-bound and can sit in the same pot for years, with
a fresh top layer of mix standing in for a repot.
