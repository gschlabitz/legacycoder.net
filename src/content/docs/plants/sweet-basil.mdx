## Origin

Basil comes from tropical Asia — a biome of warmth, sun, and steady
moisture with no cold season at all. That heritage never left the plant:
it reads anything below 50 °F as an emergency, has no dormancy to fall
back on, and so lives fast and dies with the summer.

## Care

- **Light** — full sun, sheltered and warm.
- **Temperature** — 59–86 °F; dies below 50 °F.
- **Watering** — every 2 days, daily in midsummer; it wilts dramatically
  and recovers fast.
- **Fertilizing** — every 4 weeks, April–September; a heavy feeder in a
  pot — balanced liquid feed.
- **Trimming** — every 2 weeks, May–September; pinch stem tips and flower
  spikes to keep it leafy.
- **Repotting** — yearly, April–May; an annual — divide crowded
  supermarket pots on purchase.

Supermarket basil pots are dozens of seedlings crammed together to look
lush on the shelf — split the clump into three or four pots and each will
outlive the original by months.

## Pruning

Pinching is both the harvest and the pruning: take whole stem tips just
above a leaf pair — each cut branches into two new stems — and remove
flower spikes on sight, since a basil that sets seed stops making leaves.
Plucking single leaves off the stem is the slow way to a bare plant. As an
annual it never needs more surgery than that; a plant that has outgrown
its pot is a plant ready for pesto.

## In the kitchen

The soul of pesto alla genovese, caprese, and most of the tomato canon.
Basil is at its best raw or barely warmed: tear it over a dish at the end,
since chopping and cooking both blacken it and dull the perfume. Surplus
keeps best blended with oil and frozen — dried basil is a different, sadder
herb.
