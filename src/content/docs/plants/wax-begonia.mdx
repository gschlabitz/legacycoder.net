The wax begonia is the workhorse of the genus: glossy, succulent-ish leaves,
flowers from spring until the light runs out, and no drama in between. Its
*semperflorens* name means "always flowering," and indoors on a bright
windowsill it very nearly delivers. It is one of the few bedding plants that
genuinely enjoys pot life — bring it in before frost and it carries on as a
houseplant instead of dying as an annual.

## Origin

The wax begonia's wild ancestors come from South America — Brazil and
Argentina — from a mild biome with no frost. The bedding plant keeps that
profile: it tolerates more shade than most flowering pots, roots near the
surface, and treats a bright windowsill winter as just another mild
season.

## Care

- **Light** — bright; tolerates partial shade.
- **Temperature** — 15–25 °C; no frost.
- **Watering** — every 3 days; lightly moist — water the soil, not the
  waxy leaves.
- **Fertilizing** — every 4 weeks, March–September; half-strength balanced
  liquid feed.
- **Trimming** — every 4 weeks, April–September; pinch leggy shoots and
  spent flowers to keep it compact.
- **Repotting** — yearly, March–April; a shallow pot — it roots near the
  surface.

Stem cuttings root in a glass of water within weeks, so one plant is a
subscription: refresh a tired pot from its own cuttings rather than nursing
old woody stems along.

## Pruning

Pinch leggy shoots and spent flowers every few weeks — it blooms on new
growth, so pinching costs nothing and keeps it compact. At the spring
repot it takes a hard cutback and a root trim in the same session without
complaint, going back into the same pot with fresh mix; or skip the
surgery entirely and let a water-rooted cutting take over the pot.
