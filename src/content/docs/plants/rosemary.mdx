## Origin

Rosemary grows on the sun-baked sea cliffs of the Mediterranean coasts —
the name likely comes from *ros marinus*, "dew of the sea" — and a pot on
a hot, dry balcony is the closest thing to home you can offer it.
Botanically it is now a sage, reclassified from *Rosmarinus officinalis*
to *Salvia rosmarinus* in 2017.

## Care

- **Light** — full sun.
- **Temperature** — 41–86 °F; hardy to light frost in a sheltered pot.
- **Watering** — every 7 days; let the soil dry between waterings, and
  never leave it standing in water.
- **Fertilizing** — every 8 weeks, April–August; a light feeder —
  half-strength feed is plenty.
- **Trimming** — yearly, May–June; shape lightly after flowering, never
  into bare wood.
- **Repotting** — every 2 years, March–April; gritty, fast-draining mix —
  a clay pot forgives overwatering.

Like thyme, it dies of kindness: soggy soil kills more potted rosemary
than any winter. Cold it can take; wet feet it cannot. In climates with
hard frost, overwinter the pot against a house wall or in a cold, bright
stairwell — it wants a cool rest, not a heated living room.

## Pruning

Shape it lightly after flowering and never cut into bare, leafless wood —
old wood rarely resprouts, and one hard cut is how most potted rosemary
ends. Ration the knife: rosemary tolerates a light top prune *or* a light
root prune in a year, not both. If it has outgrown its pot, pick one end
per spring and leave the other for next year.

## In the kitchen

The resinous needles stand up to long, hot cooking like almost nothing else:
roast potatoes, focaccia, lamb, and anything grilled. Use whole sprigs and
fish them out, or chop the needles fine — and halve the amount when using
dried, which concentrates the pine notes.

## Home medicine

Rosemary leaf preparations are approved in Germany's Commission E monographs
for digestive complaints and, externally, for supporting circulation —
rosemary oil shows up in warming baths and liniments. A tea of a fresh sprig
is a traditional pick-me-up; the scent alone, rubbed from a leaf, is the
quickest form of it.
