## Origin

Grows on the sun-baked sea cliffs of the Mediterranean — the name likely comes from *ros marinus*, "dew of the sea." Botanically now a sage, reclassified from *Rosmarinus officinalis* to *Salvia rosmarinus* in 2017.

## Location

Full sun on a hot, dry balcony — the closest thing to home. Takes light frost in a sheltered pot; in hard-frost climates overwinter against a house wall or in a cold, bright stairwell — it wants a cool rest, not a heated living room.

## Watering

Water weekly, let the soil dry in between, and never leave it standing in water — soggy soil kills more potted rosemary than any winter. A light feeder: half-strength feed every 8 weeks, April to August.

## Pruning

Shape lightly after flowering, never into bare wood — old wood rarely resprouts, and one hard cut ends most potted rosemary. Ration the knife: a light top prune *or* a light root prune per year, not both.

## Repotting

Every 2 years in early spring, into a gritty, fast-draining mix — a clay pot forgives overwatering. If it has outgrown its pot, prune one end per spring and leave the other for next year.

## In the kitchen

The resinous needles stand up to long, hot cooking like almost nothing else: roast potatoes, focaccia, lamb, anything grilled. Use whole sprigs and fish them out, or chop the needles fine — and halve the amount when using dried.

## Home medicine

Commission E approves rosemary leaf for digestive complaints and, externally, for supporting circulation — the oil shows up in warming baths and liniments. A tea of a fresh sprig is a traditional pick-me-up; the scent rubbed from a leaf is the quickest form of it.
