## Origin

Like all tomatoes, the species behind Husky Cherry Red comes from western
South America; the cultivar itself is a dwarf indeterminate — an odd and
useful combination. Indeterminate, so it keeps flowering and fruiting all
season rather than in one flush; dwarf, so it does it on a stocky plant
around a meter tall with short internodes that barely needs staking. That
combination is why it is sold as a patio tomato: a cherry tomato
engineered for exactly the container life the Roma merely tolerates.

## Care

- **Light** — full sun, six hours or more.
- **Temperature** — 59–86 °F; dies at frost.
- **Watering** — every 2 days; even moisture — pots dry out faster than
  the plant forgives.
- **Fertilizing** — every 2 weeks, May–September; tomato feed once
  flowering starts.
- **Trimming** — every 2 weeks, June–September; thin suckers sparingly —
  the dwarf habit does most of the work.
- **Repotting** — none; an annual — one plant per 25+ cm pot, planted
  deep.

The dwarf habit slows sucker growth but doesn't stop it — thin just enough
to keep air moving through the foliage. Cherry fruit splits after a sudden
soak, so steady watering matters more than generous watering.

## Pruning

A dwarf indeterminate wants a lighter hand than either parent type: the
short internodes already keep it stocky, so thin suckers only where the
foliage crowds — enough for air to move, no more. Pinching hard just costs
cherries. Like the Roma, it is an annual: the season ends in the compost,
and no pot ever needs to get bigger.

## In the kitchen

Cherry tomatoes are the pot-to-mouth crop; most of the harvest never reaches
the kitchen. The ones that do are sweeter and thinner-skinned than anything
from a store: halve them raw over pasta with olive oil and basil, or blister
them whole in a screaming-hot pan until they just collapse.
