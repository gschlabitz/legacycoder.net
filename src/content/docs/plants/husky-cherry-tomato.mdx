## Origin

The species comes from western South America; the cultivar is a dwarf indeterminate — it flowers and fruits all season on a stocky plant around a meter tall that barely needs staking. A cherry tomato engineered for container life.

## Location

Full sun, six hours or more, at 59–86 °F. Dies at frost.

## Watering

Every 2 days for even moisture — pots dry out faster than the plant forgives, and cherry fruit splits after a sudden soak, so steady beats generous. Tomato feed every 2 weeks once flowering starts, May to September.

## Pruning

Thin suckers sparingly, June to September, only where the foliage crowds — enough for air to move, no more. The short internodes keep it stocky on their own; pinching hard just costs cherries.

## Repotting

None — an annual: one plant per 25+ cm pot, planted deep, and the season ends in the compost. No pot ever needs to get bigger.

## In the kitchen

Cherry tomatoes are the pot-to-mouth crop; most of the harvest never reaches the kitchen. The ones that do are sweeter and thinner-skinned than anything from a store: halve them raw over pasta with olive oil and basil, or blister them whole in a screaming-hot pan until they just collapse.
