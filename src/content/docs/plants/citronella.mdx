The potted plant sold as "Citronella" is not citronella grass (*Cymbopogon*,
the source of commercial citronella oil) but a scented-leaf pelargonium
whose foliage happens to smell like it. It grows like any pelargonium:
sun-loving, drought-tolerant once established, and quick to get leggy
without a spring haircut. It is frost-tender — bring it inside before the
first cold night and give it a bright, cool window for the winter.

Cuttings root readily in early autumn, which is cheaper insurance than
hauling a large pot through the winter.

## Home medicine

The marketing claim — that the plant itself repels mosquitoes — has not
held up in trials; sitting next to one does nothing. Crushing a few leaves
and rubbing them on the skin does release citronellal and gives short-lived,
mild repellent effect, more folk remedy than substitute for a real
repellent. The scented leaves also do honest work in potpourri and drawer
sachets.
