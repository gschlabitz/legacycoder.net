## Origin

Not citronella grass (*Cymbopogon*) but a scented-leaf pelargonium whose foliage smells like it. 'Citrosum' is a garden hybrid of South African pelargoniums: sun-hungry, drought-tolerant once established, helpless against frost.

## Location

Full sun to bright indirect, 50–86 °F. Bring it inside before the first cold night and give it a bright, cool window for the winter.

## Watering

Water about every five days, letting the top few centimeters dry out first. Balanced liquid feed at half strength every four weeks, April to September.

## Pruning

Prune back hard in March as the first new shoots show — it gets leggy without. The smaller top is what makes the spring root trim safe.

## Repotting

Yearly with the spring prune: shave a few centimeters off the root ball and return it to the same pot with fresh mix. Cuttings root readily in early autumn — cheaper insurance than hauling a large pot through winter.

## Home medicine

The claim that the plant itself repels mosquitoes has not held up in trials. Crushed leaves rubbed on the skin release citronellal for a short, mild repellent effect — more folk remedy than substitute for the real thing. The scented leaves also work in potpourri and drawer sachets.
