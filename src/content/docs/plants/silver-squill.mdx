Silver squill grows from teardrop bulbs that sit *on* the soil rather than
in it — purple-skinned, water-storing, and quick to multiply into a colony
(the *socialis* in the name). The leaves are the show: silver with dark
leopard spotting on top, wine-purple underneath, plus sprays of tiny
green-and-white flowers in spring. Treat it like the succulent it almost is:
bright light, a cramped shallow pot, and restraint with the watering can.
More light means sharper spots; overwatering means mush.

## Origin

Silver squill comes from the Eastern Cape of South Africa, a dry biome it
carries with it in the exposed, water-storing bulbs: it drinks when rain
comes and sits tight when it doesn't. A generous watering schedule reads
to it as a threat, not a kindness.

## Care

- **Light** — bright, with a few hours of direct sun for good spotting.
- **Temperature** — 10–25 °C; keep above 7 °C.
- **Watering** — every 10 days; soak, then let it dry fully — near-dry
  through winter.
- **Fertilizing** — every 8 weeks, April–September; quarter-strength
  feed — it is used to poor soil.
- **Repotting** — every 3 years, March–April; a shallow pot with the bulbs
  sitting on the surface — divide the colony then.

Worth knowing with pets in the house: like many bulbs it contains cardiac
glycosides, so it belongs out of reach of nibbling cats — a shelf plant, not
a floor plant.

## Pruning

There is nothing to cut: spent leaves pull away from the bulb by hand.
When the colony crowds its pot, divide rather than prune — lift a few
bulbs at the spring repot, return the rest, and leave every bulb sitting
on the surface, never buried. The spares pot up as gifts.
