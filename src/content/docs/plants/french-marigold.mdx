## Origin

Like its "African" sibling, the French marigold is Mexican — *Tagetes
patula* comes from Mexico and Guatemala, the name marking only where
European gardens first took to it. It is the small, bushy half of the
marigold pair: low mounds covered in red, orange, and bicolor blooms all
season. Its garden reputation as a pest-deterring companion plant makes it
a traditional neighbor for the tomato pots.

## Care

- **Light** — full sun.
- **Temperature** — 59–86 °F; dies at frost.
- **Watering** — every 2 days; water when the surface begins to dry — it
  tolerates a brief dry spell.
- **Fertilizing** — every 4 weeks, May–September; half-strength feed —
  overfeeding buys leaves instead of flowers.
- **Trimming** — every 2 weeks, May–October; deadhead spent blooms to keep
  the flowers coming.
- **Repotting** — none; an annual, sown or planted fresh each spring.

Tougher and more compact than the African marigold, it shrugs off heat and
a missed watering. Seeds from spent heads keep next year's supply free.

## Pruning

Deadheading is the pruning: pinch off spent blooms before they set seed
and the mound keeps flowering until frost. Leggy midsummer plants take a
light shear-over and bounce back within two weeks. Nothing overwinters —
the season ends in the compost, seeds saved.
