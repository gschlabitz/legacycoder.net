## Origin

*Lavandula angustifolia* — "English" lavender, confusingly — is the hardy,
sweet-scented species worth growing in a pot. It grows on the
mountainsides of the western Mediterranean, in the same sun-baked scrub as
its relatives thyme and rosemary; the mountains explain the
frost-hardiness.

## Care

- **Light** — full sun.
- **Temperature** — 23–86 °F; frost-hardy in a protected pot.
- **Watering** — every 7 days; let it dry out between waterings — winter
  pots barely need any.
- **Fertilizing** — every 8 weeks, April–August; a lean diet — overfeeding
  costs fragrance and flowers.
- **Trimming** — yearly, in August; cut back by a third after flowering,
  never into leafless wood.
- **Repotting** — every 2 years, March–April; gritty, slightly alkaline
  mix — terracotta over plastic.

Harvest flower spikes just as the buds open — that is their fragrant peak —
and you can fold the harvest into the August prune.

## Pruning

Cut it back by a third right after flowering in August, and never into
leafless wood — lavender will not regrow from it, which is why the annual
prune is non-negotiable. The roots are off limits: lavender resents root
disturbance, so don't root-prune it to hold the pot size down. When a
plant turns woody and sparse after a few years, a rooted cutting inherits
the pot instead.

## Home medicine

Lavender is one of the better-evidenced calming herbs: Commission E approves
the flowers for restlessness and difficulty falling asleep, and a
standardized lavender-oil capsule (silexan) is licensed in Germany for
anxious restlessness. At home that translates to lavender tea (a teaspoon of
dried flowers, steeped ten minutes), a sachet under the pillow, or a few
drops of the essential oil in an evening bath.
