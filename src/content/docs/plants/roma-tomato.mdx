Roma is a determinate ("bush") tomato: it grows to about a meter, sets most
of its fruit in one concentrated flush, and then it is done — exactly what
you want for a batch of sauce, and why it behaves in a pot where sprawling
indeterminate varieties need scaffolding. Being determinate also means the
usual tomato ritual of pinching out side shoots mostly doesn't apply; every
sucker you leave carries fruit. Plant seedlings deep — the buried stem grows
roots along its whole length.

## Origin

The tomato species comes from western South America; 'Roma' itself is a
1955 USDA cultivar. The ancestral biome still writes the rules: full sun,
real warmth with no hint of frost, and a deep dislike of feast-or-famine
watering.

## Care

- **Light** — full sun, six hours or more.
- **Temperature** — 15–30 °C; dies at frost.
- **Watering** — every 2 days; deep and even — irregular watering causes
  blossom-end rot and split fruit.
- **Fertilizing** — every 2 weeks, May–September; tomato feed once the
  first truss has set.
- **Trimming** — every 2 weeks, June–August; remove yellowing lower
  leaves — determinate, so leave the trusses alone.
- **Repotting** — none; an annual — a fresh plant into a 30+ cm pot each
  May, buried deep.

Blossom-end rot — the black leathery patch on the fruit's base — is not a
disease but a calcium-transport failure caused by uneven watering. In a pot
that means one thing: never let it cycle between bone dry and soaked.

## Pruning

Skip the classic sucker-pinching ritual: Roma is determinate, and every
side shoot carries part of its one big flush of fruit — pinching just
costs you sauce. The only cuts are housekeeping: take off yellowing
leaves below the lowest truss every couple of weeks to keep air moving
through the pot. As an annual, it ends the season in the compost, not in
a bigger pot.

## In the kitchen

Romas are dense, seed-poor, and low on juice — mediocre in a salad and
exactly right in the pan. They reduce into sauce in half the time of a slicing
tomato, and they are the classic choice for canning whole, oven-drying, and
long-simmered *sugo*. A tray of halved Romas, roasted flat with olive oil and
salt, is the shortest path from pot to pasta.
