## Origin

The species comes from western South America; 'Roma' is a 1955 USDA cultivar. A determinate ("bush") tomato: about a meter tall, one concentrated flush of fruit, then done — which is why it behaves in a pot.

## Location

Full sun, six hours or more. Thrives at 59–86 °F and dies at frost.

## Watering

Water deep and even every 2 days — irregular watering causes blossom-end rot and split fruit, so never let it cycle between bone dry and soaked. Tomato feed every 2 weeks once the first truss has set, May to September.

## Pruning

Skip sucker-pinching: Roma is determinate, every side shoot carries fruit, and pinching just costs sauce. Only remove yellowing leaves below the lowest truss every couple of weeks to keep air moving.

## Repotting

None — it's an annual. Plant a fresh seedling deep into a 30+ cm pot each May; the buried stem roots along its whole length. Season over, it goes to the compost.

## In the kitchen

Romas are dense, seed-poor, and low on juice — mediocre in a salad, exactly right in the pan. They reduce into sauce in half the time of a slicing tomato and are the classic choice for canning, oven-drying, and long-simmered *sugo*. Halved and roasted flat with olive oil and salt, they're the shortest path from pot to pasta.
