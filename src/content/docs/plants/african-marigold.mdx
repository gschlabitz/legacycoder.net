## Origin

"African" in name only: *Tagetes erecta* comes from Mexico and Central
America, where the Aztecs grew it long before trade routes misfiled it —
it is still the flower of the Día de los Muertos. This is the tall
marigold of the genus, upright stems carrying big pompon blooms in orange
and gold. An annual from a warm, sunny biome, it runs one loud season and
ends at frost.

## Care

- **Light** — full sun.
- **Temperature** — 59–86 °F; dies at frost.
- **Watering** — every 2 days; water when the surface begins to dry — the
  soil, not the flowers.
- **Fertilizing** — every 4 weeks, May–September; half-strength feed —
  overfeeding buys leaves instead of flowers.
- **Trimming** — every 2 weeks, May–October; deadhead spent blooms to keep
  the flowers coming.
- **Repotting** — none; an annual, sown or planted fresh each spring.

The big blooms hold rain and can rot in a wet spell — water at the base
and pick off soggy flower heads. Seeds saved from spent heads come up
easily the next spring.

## Pruning

Deadheading is the pruning: snap or snip spent blooms before they set
seed, and the plant keeps spending its energy on new buds — an annual
that finishes seeding considers its work done. Pinch the young plant's
tip once for a bushier frame, and that's the whole toolkit; there is no
autumn cutback, only the compost at frost.
