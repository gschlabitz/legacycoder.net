"German" thyme is the winter-hardy strain of common thyme — the one that
survives outside on a balcony where French thyme gives up. It is a small
Mediterranean shrub at heart: it wants sun, lean soil, and neglect. The
fastest way to kill a potted thyme is kindness, in the form of water and
rich potting mix.

## Care schedule

<PlantCare care={frontmatter.plant.care} />

Trim it back by a third after flowering to keep it from going woody and
bald in the middle. Never cut into old, leafless wood — it rarely resprouts.

## In the kitchen

The backbone herb of stocks, stews, and anything braised. Whole sprigs go in
early and come out before serving; stripped leaves can be added any time.
Thyme dries better than almost any herb — hang surplus sprigs upside down
for a week and the flavor keeps for months.

## Home medicine

Thyme tea is the classic German cough remedy: thymol, its main essential
oil, is a proven expectorant and mild antiseptic, and thyme preparations are
approved for coughs and bronchitis in Germany's Commission E monographs.
Steep a teaspoon of dried (or a fresh sprig of) thyme in hot water for ten
minutes, with honey to taste.
