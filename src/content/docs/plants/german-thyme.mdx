## Origin

"German" thyme is the winter-hardy strain of common thyme, native to the western Mediterranean, where it grows as a small shrub on dry, sunny ground.

## Location

Full sun, 41–86 °F, frost-hardy — it survives outside on a balcony where French thyme gives up. It wants lean soil and neglect.

## Watering

Every 10 days; let the soil dry out completely between waterings. A light feeder: half-strength feed or a compost top-up every 8 weeks, April to August. The fastest way to kill a potted thyme is kindness.

## Pruning

One real cut, after flowering in July: shear back by a third to keep it from going woody and bald in the middle. Stay above green growth — it will not resprout from old, leafless wood.

## Repotting

Every 2 years in March or April, into gritty, fast-draining mix; terracotta helps. A light root prune at the first spring growth keeps it in the same pot — just keep any same-year top and root work gentle.

## In the kitchen

The backbone herb of stocks, stews, and anything braised. Whole sprigs go in early and come out before serving; stripped leaves any time. Thyme dries better than almost any herb — hang surplus sprigs upside down for a week and the flavor keeps for months.

## Home medicine

Thyme tea is the classic German cough remedy: thymol, its main essential oil, is a proven expectorant and mild antiseptic, approved for coughs and bronchitis in Germany's Commission E monographs. Steep a teaspoon of dried (or a fresh sprig of) thyme in hot water for ten minutes, with honey to taste.
