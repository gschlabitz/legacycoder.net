Coriander is an annual on a deadline: warmth, long days, or a dry spell all
flip it from leaf production to a flower stalk, and once it bolts the leaves
turn sparse and soapy-thin. The pot-growing strategy is not to fight this
but to plan for it — sow a pinch of seed directly in the pot (the taproot
resents transplanting, which is why supermarket pots sulk), harvest for a
few weeks, and have the next pot already sown. In summer heat, give it
morning sun and midday shade to stretch each sowing.

There is no trimming schedule because harvesting *is* the trimming — cut
outer leaves and let the center keep producing, or shear the whole pot at
its peak and resow.

## In the kitchen

One plant, two spices. The fresh leaves (cilantro) are the raw, last-second
herb of salsa, curry, phở, and guacamole — cooking erases them. If a pot
bolts before you get there, let it: the round seeds that follow are
coriander proper, warm and citrusy, for toasting and grinding into curry
bases and spice rubs. Whether the leaves taste bright or like soap is partly
genetic — if you drew the soap card, the seeds are your consolation prize.
