## Origin

A cool-season annual from the eastern Mediterranean and western Asia: up with the rains, seed before the summer drought. Heat, long days, or a dry spell flip it from leaves to a flower stalk — bolting is its original strategy, not a flaw.

## Location

Bright with morning sun, shaded from midday heat, 50–77 °F. In summer, morning sun and midday shade stretch each sowing.

## Watering

Keep it evenly moist — drought stress triggers bolting. Half-strength balanced liquid feed every four weeks, March to September.

## Pruning

Harvesting is the pruning: cut outer leaves and let the center keep producing, or shear the whole pot at its peak and resow. Once it bolts, let it finish — the seeds are the second harvest.

## Repotting

None — the taproot hates transplanting, which is why supermarket pots sulk. Sow directly in the final pot and resow every few weeks.

## In the kitchen

One plant, two spices. Fresh leaves (cilantro) go raw at the last second into salsa, curry, phở, and guacamole — cooking erases them. The seeds of a bolted pot are coriander proper, warm and citrusy, for toasting and grinding into curry bases and spice rubs.
